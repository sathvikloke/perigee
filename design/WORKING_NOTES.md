# UI direction working record

This is a new operational surface for curious learners, not a long marketing page. The core mechanism is comparing two real observations while their dates, wavelengths and quality stay visible, then writing a supported conclusion. The user's “you handle UI design” delegates creative selection; the existing product decisions answer the init questions, so no additional interview or approval pause is needed under Bloody Coconut's coordination rules.

## Grounded candidates before the seed

1. Observatory comparison desk: large sky plate, chronological contact strip and a compact evidence notebook; muted blue instrument surfaces and rounded controls.
2. Museum discovery station: a clear question, two equally weighted observation plates and a guided decision; friendly bright field colors with restrained scientific labels.
3. Photographic contact sheet: observation selection through a real image sequence, exact metadata beneath each frame and an adjacent editing workspace.
4. Field-guide atlas: a target index, coordinate orientation and a large observation window with notes; accessible wayfinding and a coherent map vocabulary.
5. Annotated science publication: figure-led comparisons, visible source captions and a structured claim beside the figure; modern reading typography with useful editorial rhythm.
6. Classroom experiment bench: compare, check, explain as three compact persistent zones; controls stay with the evidence they affect.
7. Mission activity map: the question and investigation progress organize the surface around a large comparison stage, with a clear outcome at the end.

The usual rut is a neon space landing page with decorative planets and tiny product screenshots. Its predictable opposite is a beige serif editorial site with almost no visible science. Neither is required. Familiar operational controls remain eligible when they serve the task; provider novelty defaults do not override audience clarity.

## Vault findings that affect the build

The Downloads/UI Design vault contains only the default welcome note. The active substantive vault is `Documents/Codex/2026-11-13/rand/outputs/3d-design-research/vault`, especially `10 - UI Design Principles`.

- Visual direction: derive identity from the actual comparison mechanism, not a generic space mood.
- Spatial hierarchy: one large image region, a distinct evidence-writing region and compact metadata; avoid redundant cards around every label.
- Interaction states: preserve notes and selected evidence through loading, failure and retry.
- Responsive recomposition: keep the frame selector, selected date and preview together on phones. Move extended evidence detail below them.
- Controls and consequences: time stepping changes the visible frame immediately in the same region; wavelength and quality remain adjacent.
- Quality budgets and rendering choice: semantic HTML/CSS and real image assets are sufficient. A decorative 3D scene would consume sprint time without improving the comparison.

These are selected relevant notes, not a claim to have reviewed every page in the vault. Vault content is read-only; no changes are made to the user's notes.
